# Foundation Year study guide sample

This is an original, synthetic four-week academic preparation sequence for a learner entering a science Foundation Year. The target institution and syllabus are unspecified, so alignment is provisional. The learner interface and instruction language are English. Each week assumes four 45-minute independent sessions and one feedback opportunity. A project using this guide should adapt duration to the institution's actual timetable. The table below is the sequence overview. The numbered weeks then provide the teaching and practice. Text gives every quantity conveyed by a possible graph, and no instruction depends on colour.

## Objectives and sequence

| ID | Observable goal | Prior knowledge | Evidence of learning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| FY-O1 | Convert length and time units and explain each conversion | Decimal arithmetic | Correct conversion with a unit check |
| FY-O2 | Compute and interpret a rate from a table | FY-O1, division | Calculation and sentence about what the rate means |
| FY-O3 | Evaluate whether a claim follows from a small data set | FY-O2, reading a table | A claim, quoted data and a limitation |
| FY-O4 | Write a short evidence-based explanation using a source | FY-O3, paraphrasing | Paragraph with evidence and an explicit inference |

Week 1 covers units and rates through worked examples and guided practice. Week 2 adds tables, graphs and a second representation of rate. Week 3 introduces source evaluation and claim limits. Week 4 returns to the first three objectives through cumulative retrieval and asks for a short transfer task. A diagnostic on day one checks unit conversion and proportional reasoning. Its results may change support and pacing without removing an objective.

## Week 1 lesson extract, FY-S1

A rate compares change in one quantity with change in another. If a cart travels 120 metres in 30 seconds at a constant speed, its speed is `120 m / 30 s = 4 m/s`. Dividing both numbers by 30 leaves one second as the time unit. The answer means the cart travels four metres in each second under this model. If speed changes during the journey, the same calculation gives average speed over the 30 seconds.

Guided check. Another cart travels 150 metres in 50 seconds. Write the division, include the units and explain the result.

Independent check. A cyclist covers 1.8 kilometres in six minutes. Convert to metres and seconds, then calculate average speed.

## Week 2 connection, FY-S2

| Time in seconds | Distance in metres |
| ---: | ---: |
| 0 | 0 |
| 10 | 20 |
| 20 | 40 |
| 30 | 60 |

The distance rises by 20 metres for each 10-second interval. The rate is `20 / 10 = 2 m/s`. A graph of these data would put time on the horizontal axis and distance on the vertical axis, with the points `(0,0)`, `(10,20)`, `(20,40)` and `(30,60)` on a straight line. This table provides the values without requiring a graph. Ask whether `2 m/s` describes a change over the recorded intervals or guarantees the object can maintain that speed after 30 seconds.

## Week 3 source task, FY-S3

Use the table above as the only source. Write a two-sentence claim about the recorded motion, citing two rows. Then write one limitation. This assesses FY-O3 and prepares FY-O4.

## Week 4 cumulative and transfer task, FY-S4

Without looking back, convert 0.9 kilometres to metres and five minutes to seconds. A delivery robot then covers 0.9 kilometres in five minutes. Calculate average speed and explain whether that single result tells us its speed at every instant. Ask for a different measurement that would help detect speed changes.

## Assessment and feedback

The first-attempt response to FY-S4 is recorded separately from any hint-assisted correction. A later task with different values checks retention and a new context checks transfer. The user or tutor should decide the delay from actual study time and observed responses. The teacher key and rubric are in a separate file.

## Traceability and review note

FY-O1 is taught in FY-S1 and checked in FY-S4. FY-O2 is taught in FY-S1 and FY-S2 and checked in FY-S4. FY-O3 is taught in FY-S2 and FY-S3 and checked in FY-S4. FY-O4 is introduced in FY-S3 and requires a fuller writing task before an actual course could claim complete coverage. No learner outcome is claimed.
