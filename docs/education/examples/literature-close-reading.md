# Literature close reading sample

This original passage is written for a synthetic secondary-level lesson. It is not aligned to a named national syllabus. The instruction and response language are English. The source is reproduced in full so learners can cite exact words without needing outside access. The passage and task use text alone, with no colour or image cues.

## Source, LIT-T1

> Mara left the lamp burning in the empty room. At the gate she stopped, felt for the key in her coat, and found it where she had put it. Across the street the bakery was opening. She turned back once, although the window showed only her own reflection.

## Objective and explanation

LIT-O1 asks learners to support an interpretation of Mara's action with two precise details and to acknowledge another plausible reading. An interpretation is a claim about the passage. A detail is a piece of language from the passage that helps justify the claim. The final sentence is useful because “turned back once” describes an action and the reflection complicates what she sees. The passage leaves Mara's motive unresolved.

One reading is that Mara is reluctant to leave. The lamp left burning and her turn back suggest lingering attention to the room. A second reading is that she is checking a practical detail. She explicitly feels for the key, and the open bakery may indicate that she is beginning an ordinary errand. Neither reading proves a hidden biography. The text supports a reasoned comparison.

## Practice item, LIT-I1

Write 100–150 words explaining why Mara turns back. Use two details from LIT-T1 and discuss one alternative interpretation. You may defend either reading above or another reading grounded in the source. Use exact words or clear paraphrases as evidence. The teacher rubric and model response are kept in a separate file.
