# Literature teacher key and rubric

Keep this original, synthetic key separate from `literature-close-reading.md` when giving LIT-I1 to learners.

LIT-I1 awards four points. Give one point for a clear interpretation, one for two accurate details from LIT-T1, one for explaining how those details support the interpretation, and one for a plausible alternative with a textual reason. A text-grounded alternative may earn full credit even when it differs from the model. Do not infer Mara's unspoken feelings or a specific backstory as source facts.

Model response. “Mara may be hesitant to leave. She keeps the lamp on in an empty room and turns back at the gate, which could suggest she still has the room in mind. Yet the passage also shows her checking the key in her coat. She may simply be making sure she can return later. The reflection in the window prevents us from knowing what she hopes to see.”

The model cites the lamp, turning back, key and reflection. A response asserting that Mara is fleeing a specific event would need source evidence the passage does not provide. Reviewers should apply the same four criteria to every defensible interpretation. The passage is original to this example and requires no external quotation access.
