# Learning-science evidence register

The bundled [registry](../../agent-plugins/shared/education/evidence.json) is a selective, inspected baseline for the education skills. It is a seed set rather than a systematic review. Inspection took place on 2026-11-25. Search terms included `IES Organizing Instruction and Study`, `Dunlosky effective learning techniques`, `retrieval practice spacing meta-analysis`, `CAST UDL 3.0`, `learning styles matching hypothesis`, `worked examples expertise reversal`, and `AI tutoring unassisted delayed learning`. The search used official IES and CAST pages, original journal or abstract pages, PubMed bibliographic records, and the NBER working-paper page. Sources entered the baseline when their inspected text bore on an operational rule or a counterexample. The search did not establish exhaustive coverage, and several full texts remain unappraised.

The 2007 IES guide rates delayed spacing and alternating worked examples with practice as moderate evidence, re-exposure quizzes as strong evidence, and pre-questions as minimal evidence. Its recommendations concern different interventions, so one guide does not confer the same certainty on all three. The 2019 IES technology guide rates timely, targeted feedback for postsecondary learners as moderate evidence. [IES study guide](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/WWC/PracticeGuide/1/Published), [IES technology guide](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/WWC/Docs/PracticeGuide/wwc-using-tech-postsecondary.pdf)

Dunlosky and colleagues review ten techniques, but the inspected article page exposed the abstract and bibliographic details only. The baseline does not copy unverified detailed ratings from the full review. The 2018 open article on complex, high-element-interactivity material reports delayed outcomes favoring extra worked-example study over early problem solving in its reported context. That boundary cautions against applying retrieval-first advice to every novice task. [Dunlosky et al.](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/journals/pspi/1529100612453266/), [complex-material study](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2018.01483/full)

The learning-styles evidence contains a conflict worth preserving. A 2008 review found little adequate crossover evidence for preference-matched instruction. A 2024 meta-analysis reported a positive pooled average, while its abstract says only 26% of outcome measures showed a crossover benefit for at least two styles and cites low study quality. These records support avoiding universal style-label prescriptions while allowing learners to express preferences and access needs. Full-text risk-of-bias appraisal remains open. [Pashler et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26162104/), [Clinton-Lisell and Litzinger](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39055994/)

CAST's 2024 UDL 3.0 guidelines provide design options for engagement, representation, and action and expression. They are treated as a professional framework for access decisions. A particular adaptation still needs a check against the learning objective and any assessed construct. [CAST UDL Guidelines](https://udlguidelines.cast.org/)

Two recent sources sharpen the distinction between practice performance and independent learning. A 2026 NBER working-paper abstract reports a randomized middle-school mathematics experiment with more than 6,000 learners. AI assistance improved correction after mistakes, while mastery progression alone did not improve delayed learning and the clearest delayed AI gains were marginal and concentrated on practiced material. A 2026 classroom intervention abstract reports a repeated-item retrieval benefit, no overall transfer advantage, and mixed exploratory transfer measures. These findings are provisional because the inspected evidence was abstract-level and the settings are narrow. [AI tutoring experiment](https://www.nber.org/papers/w35621), [spaced retrieval classroom intervention](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42803776/)

The 2014 AERA, APA and NCME testing standards require evidence for intended score interpretations and uses, including reliability, precision and fairness. ETS's evidence-centered-design report provides a conceptual way to connect a claim, observable response and task, while its inspected page does not itself validate a generated test. An IES formative-assessment review screened 76 relevant elementary studies and identified 23 it considered rigorous enough for causal confidence. The summary reports differences by subject and by who directed the assessment. This supports targeted formative probes while leaving a single response insufficient to establish an error's cause. [Testing standards](https://www.testingstandards.net/uploads/7/6/6/4/76643089/standards_2014edition.pdf), [ETS design report](https://www.ets.org/research/policy_research_reports/publications/report/2003/hsgs.html), [IES formative review](https://ies.ed.gov/use-work/resource-library/report/descriptive-study/formative-assessment-and-elementary-school-student-academic-achievement-review-evidence)

Corbett and Anderson's knowledge-tracing paper describes probabilistic estimates of procedural knowledge from tutor interactions. A longitudinal EDM paper finds model behavior can shift across cohorts and years. Neither establishes that a handful of assisted events yields a calibrated mastery probability for a new learner. For sparse events, describe attempts, hints, missing observations, denominators and delayed outcomes before considering a predictive model. SoLAR's higher-education policy article argues for transparency, equity and learner agency in analytics. It does not determine local child-data requirements, which need jurisdiction-specific checks. [Knowledge-tracing paper](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01099821), [longitudinal evaluation](https://educationaldatamining.org/EDM2023/proceedings/2023.EDM-short-papers.28/index.html), [SoLAR policy article](https://www.solaresearch.org/2021/03/leveraging-the-potential-of-learning-analytics-in-higher-education-requires-inclusive-policies-and-practices/)

## Appraisal and conflict procedure

For each new source, record the bibliographic identity, retrieval date, version, access level, design, population, setting, subject, age, intervention, comparator, outcome, follow-up, limitations, bias, consistency, directness and certainty. An inaccessible or abstract-only study keeps unknown fields explicit. Distinguish a study's design from the strength of its evidence and the applicability to the current learner. Compare counterevidence by outcome and follow-up first, then design and bias, learner/task match and replication. Preserve contradictory findings in the registry. Propose rule changes with a fixed baseline and held-out checks through maintainer review. A model's favorable rating is only a process observation.

## Model and strategy selection

Backward Design and constructive alignment are useful for keeping goals, teaching and assessment connected. ADDIE and SAM guide project process at different levels of iteration. Bloom labels can help vary cognitive demand, while a verb alone does not prove that an objective is measurable. Merrill, Rosenshine, worked examples and scaffolding may inform novice instruction when the task benefits from explicit modeling. Four-component instructional design may help complex procedural skills when its implementation burden is justified. Cognitive-load and multimedia ideas warrant checking whether a diagram or narration serves the concept. Retrieval, spacing and feedback support later checks after suitable initial teaching. Interleaving needs a clear distinction learners can practice. Mastery, metacognition and UDL require context and validation. None of these models supplies an automatic complete lesson template, a guaranteed outcome, a fixed spacing calendar or a universal mastery percentage.

## Catalog coverage and unresolved searches

The named catalog checks were discovery only. ERIC's home page timed out in the browsing tool. The EEF Toolkit landing page exposed only a brief summary. The AECT reference library listed journals and books, while the instructional-design models catalog listed prescriptive models. The EdTech Books landing page had no readable text in the browsing result. The Learning Scientists home page exposed its purpose and navigation, while the CAST, IES and testing-standards sources above provided usable primary material. The AECT, model, book and blog catalogs did not enter the evidence registry as causal sources. [EEF Toolkit](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/teaching-learning-toolkit), [AECT library](https://aect.org/aect/reference-library), [model catalog](https://www.instructionaldesign.org/models/), [EdTech Books](https://edtechbooks.org/foundations_of_learn), [Learning Scientists](https://www.learningscientists.org/)

Six further named seed leads received bounded source checks on 2026-11-25. Merrill's 2002 paper was accessible as a publisher abstract, which describes five prescriptive principles and labels its work preliminary. Rosenshine's 2012 AFT article was accessible in full as a nine-page PDF and combines cognitive science, classroom observation and cognitive-support research. Its particular classroom practices should remain context-sensitive. The Cambridge *Handbook of Multimedia Learning* publisher page identified the third edition, editors Mayer and Fiorella, and 46 chapters, while the displayed chapter content required access. These three sources can guide model selection and further study without promoting a universal lesson rule from the inspected material. [Merrill article](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02505024), [Rosenshine PDF](https://www.aft.org/sites/default/files/Rosenshine.pdf), [Cambridge handbook](https://doi.org/10.1017/9781108894333)

The EEF metacognition evidence-review PDF was accessible in full. Its November 2025 internal review draws on 297 studies in the Toolkit and says it cannot identify one definitively best teaching strategy. It also reports gaps for non-cognitive outcomes. This reinforces context-specific modelling, planning and reflection without assigning a fixed effect to every technique. Brill's DOI for the sixth-edition *Survey of Instructional Design Models* returned a 403 in the browsing tool, while the DOI search result exposed a publisher summary describing 12 models. The ASCD publisher page for Wiggins and McTighe's expanded second-edition *Understanding by Design* exposed bibliographic details, an overview and table of contents, including Backward Design. Neither book was treated as causal evidence. [EEF evidence review](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/evidence-reviews/metacognition-and-self-regulation-guidance-report), [Brill survey DOI](https://doi.org/10.1163/9789004533691), [ASCD book page](https://www.ascd.org/books/understanding-by-design-expanded-2nd-edition)

Remaining research gaps include full-text appraisal of recent AI tutoring studies and paywalled model books, direct evidence for language and humanities transfer, jurisdiction-specific privacy and exam requirements, psychometric reliability for locally created assessments, and deployment validation of knowledge-tracing models. This register is not an exhaustive review or an empirical validation of the new skills.
